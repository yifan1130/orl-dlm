Abstract

Residual context carries predictive distributions across denoising steps. But an effective learning recipe must account for the fact that these predictions—and the trajectories they induce—change as the model learns.

ORL closes this loop. It trains on fresh, verified trajectories from the current model, replays the exact residual inputs encountered during generation, and uses adjacent entropy contraction to encourage more decisive refinement. A co-designed training system makes this practical at scale.

Across QwenDLM, LLaDA-2.1-mini, and DiffusionGemma, ORL improves parallel decoding efficiency. With QwenDLM, it delivers up to 6.2× higher TPF and approximately 5× throughput at comparable or better quality.

Read the paper’s full abstract +

Residual context is emerging as a key ingredient for unlocking the speed–quality Pareto frontier of diffusion language models (DLMs) by carrying forward predictive distributions across denoising steps. Yet turning this seemingly simple mechanism of computation recirculation into an effective and efficient learning paradigm is far from simple: there is no clear recipe for how residual context should be learned. We introduce ORL, a framework for on-policy residual learning in DLMs. Algorithmically, we identify that residual learning is inherently on-policy. ORL learns directly from these model-induced residual trajectories on the fly and introduces an entropy-contraction objective that encourages sharpening predictions as the model revisits its own intermediate predictions. System-wise, exact residual supervision requires capturing, retaining, and repeatedly processing many trajectory-dependent intermediate states. ORL co-designs the learning algorithm with asynchronous residual capture and transport, specialized block-diffusion attention, and packed compute-balanced training, making on-policy residual learning practical at scale. On a 4B DLM converted from Qwen3-4B, ORL improves tokens per forward (TPF) by up to 6.2× and end-to-end throughput by approximately 5× at comparable or better generation quality. On a single H100, ORL-trained models achieve a stronger speed–quality Pareto frontier than autoregressive models accelerated with EAGLE3 and DFlashV1 speculative decoding, under both strict and relaxed verification. On LLaDA-2.1-mini, 36 H100-hours and 4.2M self-generated tokens, about 10× less post-training data than prior LLaDA parallel-decoding recipes, yield up to 1.5× higher TPF and up to 3× higher throughput at matched quality.

Why learn residuals on-policy?

Residuals change the states a model visits. Learning from them requires fresh trajectories, an explicit objective for refinement speed, and a system that can replay the exact inputs.

On-Policy Residual Learning

01

Stay on-policy

Generate fresh trajectories after every update. Train on the residual-conditioned states the current model actually visits.

02

Reward refinement

Supervise intermediate predictions with verified outputs. Apply an entropy-contraction loss only when progress falls short.

03

Keep the loop stable

Control residual-pathway updates, gate supervision by uncertainty, and anchor confident predictions with capped reverse KL.

Learning how fast to refine

AEC asks the next state to reduce normalized entropy by a fraction ρ. Its hinge loss becomes zero once that target is met. Each state’s entropy is averaged over its own unresolved positions.

Efficient Exact Residual Training

01

Asynchronous state capture

Snapshot states during generation and transfer them off the critical path. Colocated training reuses GPU-resident residual pages via CUDA IPC.

02

Packed, balanced training

Reuse clean context, pack variable-length states, and distribute work by estimated compute cost while preserving response-normalized losses.

03

Staircase attention

A specialized Triton kernel enforces block-diffusion visibility and skips invisible tiles without allocating a dense attention mask.

Related Work

Residual context

RCD and Looped Diffusion motivate reusing intermediate predictions. ORL adds a recipe for learning from the evolving residual trajectories themselves.

Trajectory supervision

TraceRL motivates slicing and reusing clean context during training. ORL preserves exact residual inputs and directly trains refinement speed.

Speculative decoding

EAGLE3 and DFlash accelerate a fixed AR target. ORL jointly changes the DLM’s quality and parallelism, exposing a different optimization dimension.
